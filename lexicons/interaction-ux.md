# Interaction & UX Heuristics Lexicon

## About this document

Decision rules for the perception, cognition, behaviour, and correction of the
human surface: how users group, miss, expect, scan, remember, choose, navigate,
act, and correct. Referenced, not read end to end: a design plan, implementation
diff, or walkthrough cites a rule by ID where the decision happens, and this
file holds the claim behind it. It does not own visual tokens, contrast, or
spacing (engineering `UI-*`) or graph partition (`GRPH-*`); it owns the meaning
of spacing, similarity, placement, action, and error relative to human vision
and memory.

Division of responsibility (per the expansion brief): design-aesthetics chooses
visual direction; interaction-ux governs perception, behaviour, and correction;
engineering `UI-*` implements the interface correctly; accessibility constrains
every layer; ml-systems governs model evidence, uncertainty, and operations.

What it covers, eight populated families:

- [**PERC**](interaction-ux.md#fam-perc): Gestalt grouping, perception, attention, figure/ground
- [**COG**](interaction-ux.md#fam-cog): memory, cognitive load, recognition vs recall, decision cost
- [**NAV**](interaction-ux.md#fam-nav): information architecture, wayfinding, information scent
- [**INT**](interaction-ux.md#fam-int): feedback, direct manipulation, undo, modes, state transitions
- [**FORM**](interaction-ux.md#fam-form): data entry, validation, recovery, destructive actions
- [**HAI**](interaction-ux.md#fam-hai): human-AI interaction, calibrated reliance, correction
- [**VIZ**](interaction-ux.md#fam-viz): data-to-channel encoding (quantity and magnitude channels, glyph
  design, colour for data and colormaps, layering, comparison and coordinated
  views, node-link grammar)
- [**UXR**](interaction-ux.md#fam-uxr): usability research and measurement (task success, time-on-task,
  errors, self-report, issue severity, participant count, validation level)

[`VIZ`](interaction-ux.md#fam-viz) owns how evidence is drawn; it does not own uncertainty display, which stays
with `HAI-08` (uncertainty at the decision granularity). Scope boundary:
decision heuristics for the human surface, not an HCI textbook. What the
interface must do so a person groups, notices, remembers, acts, and corrects
correctly, not the psychology proof beneath it; the mechanism stays in the
source.

Interface patterns rest on Tidwell, Brewer, and Valencia's
[*Designing Interfaces*](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) and Johnson's
[*Designing with the Mind in Mind*](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind);
measurement on Albert and Tullis's
[*Measuring the User Experience*](../SOURCES.md#src-measuring-the-ux-albert-tullis);
visualization on Ware's
[*Information Visualization: Perception for Design*](../SOURCES.md#src-information-visualization-ware)
and Munzner's [*Visualization Analysis and Design*](../SOURCES.md#src-visualization-analysis-design-munzner).
Human-AI surface rules also draw on Shneiderman's
[*Human-Centered AI*](../SOURCES.md#src-human-centered-ai) and Monarch's
[*Human-in-the-Loop Machine Learning*](../SOURCES.md#src-human-in-the-loop-ml).

## 1. PERC: Perception, Gestalt grouping, attention<a name="fam-perc"></a>

| ID | Trigger | Rule | Answers | T·P | Src |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PERC-01<a name="perc-01"></a> | The gap between a label and its control is ≥ the gap to a neighbour, or a one-item group box | **Proximity binds meaning**: space-encode groups so each label sits nearer its target than any distractor; the eye reads the wrong association from the spacing alone (Gestalt proximity) (↔ eng [[UI-03]](engineering.md#ui-03), design [[LAY-03]](design-aesthetics.md#lay-03) all three read grouping from spacing alone, so the gap is the claim) | Is each label closer to its control than to any other? | S·r | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| PERC-02<a name="perc-02"></a> | Identical visual style on unrelated items, or a critical status lacks a distinctive feature | **Similarity encodes semantics**: a shared look promises shared meaning, so unify the look only for shared behaviour and give status a distinctive feature, or a similarity pass invents false groups (Gestalt similarity) | Would a Gestalt similarity pass invent a wrong group? | S·r | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| PERC-03<a name="perc-03"></a> | A finished layout claims a multi-element grouping | **Seven-principle Gestalt audit**: before shipping a grouped display, check proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, symmetry, figure-ground, and common-fate for relations you did not intend (↔ grph [[GRPH-22]](graph-theory.md#grph-22), eng [[DOM-03]](engineering.md#dom-03); ↔ [[HAI-05]](interaction-ux.md#hai-05) a perceived autonomy boundary the system does not hold; ↔ [[SEC-01]](security.md#sec-01) trust changes at every crossing so an unenforced boundary is only in the mind; ↔ [[CLM-03]](business-marketing.md#clm-03) a policy promise against a divergent implementation; ↔ [[WEB-08]](security.md#web-08) route check without object auth is a boundary the eye trusts and the server does not) | Does any Gestalt principle invent a relation we don't mean? | S·r | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| PERC-04<a name="perc-04"></a> | An ambiguous figure, icon, or chart admits two or more parses that differ by task or prime | **Disambiguate or prime**: resolve a multi-parse display with convention, a legend, or a prime, and test that two differently-primed cohorts parse it the same; otherwise the same pixels drive opposite actions (↔ ml [[MLDATA-03]](ml-systems.md#mldata-03) resolve label multiplicity in writing before "gold" is claimed) | Do two primed cohorts parse this display the same way? | B·r | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| PERC-05<a name="perc-05"></a> | New critical information or an error appears away from the fovea after an action | **Fovea-first feedback**: place post-action status at or near the user's fovea with a distinctive mark; the periphery is low-resolution, so the change is missed and the user re-submits into a blank (↔ a11y non-colour status) | Where is the user's fovea at the moment this appears? | B·w | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| PERC-06<a name="perc-06"></a> | A target must be found among many distractors that differ only by fine shape or detail | **Make it pop**: encode the target with weight, colour, motion, or gross shape the periphery can detect, or visual search stays linear in the number of items (↔ a11y [[A11Y-06]](accessibility.md#a11y-06) a second non-colour channel is what makes the target findable without colour vision) | Does the target pop without a foveal scan? | S·w | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| PERC-07<a name="perc-07"></a> | A strong interrupt (modal, blink, beep, confirm) fires on a high-frequency path | **Don't habituate alarms**: reserve strong attention-getting methods for rare, critical, irreversible events and suppress the routine, or users learn to dismiss the one that matters (↔ [[HAI-13]](interaction-ux.md#hai-13) the AI-verify-cue instance: a standing "verify" cue on every fluent output is the alarm that habituates, so the last error detector goes quiet; ↔ eng [[OBS-04]](engineering.md#obs-04) a signal that always fires goes quiet in the reader, [[AGT-16]](engineering.md#agt-16) constant confirmation trains the reflex press) | Is this interrupt rarer and worse than the routine noise around it? | B·p | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| PERC-08<a name="perc-08"></a> | A spatial description assigns figure and ground from object size or mobility alone. | **Check the selected figure and ground**: compare that assignment with the speaker's actual encoding, because size and mobility are tendencies rather than guaranteed roles, and a larger or less mobile object can be the foregrounded anchor. ↔ [[PERC-03]](interaction-ux.md#perc-03) audit unintended Gestalt grouping in a finished layout; this row checks the speaker's encoding, not the pixel groups. ↔ [[PERC-04]](interaction-ux.md#perc-04) resolve two parses of one figure, icon, or chart; this row checks which object the description treats as the reference. | Which object did the speaker encode as the reference, and what in the description supports that? | S·r | [Spatial Semiotics and Spatial Mental Models, ch. 2](../SOURCES.md#src-thiering-spatial-semiotics) |
| PERC-09<a name="perc-09"></a> | Unequal page regions carry equal visual weight, a heading visually groups the wrong content, or page regions are too indistinct for a scanner to skip an irrelevant area. | **Hierarchy for scanners**: make importance, grouping, and nesting visible before the user reads, and give distinct purposes distinct regions, because otherwise people scanning the page must invent the organization item by item. ↔ [[PERC-03]](interaction-ux.md#perc-03) a seven-principle Gestalt audit looks for groupings you did not intend; this row requires the hierarchy to show importance, nesting, and region purpose. ↔ [[PERC-06]](interaction-ux.md#perc-06) pop-out encoding finds one target among fine-detail distractors; it does not structure the page for scanning. | After a brief glance, can a visitor point out the primary task and the page's major regions? | S·w | [Don't Make Me Think, Revisited, ch. 3](../SOURCES.md#src-dont-make-me-think-krug) |
| PERC-10<a name="perc-10"></a> | The message is already stated, but a routine or unchanged status still stacks more than one of animation, sound, and vibration, or new chrome repeats a message an existing control, cursor, or bar could carry. | **Least useful feedback**: use the smallest clear signal, prefer an existing element for routine status, and raise prominence only with importance, because redundant effects clutter the screen and train people to ignore alerts. A critical alert, a screenless device, or an eyes-busy task may need another channel, and nonessential audio should be silenceable. ↔ [[PERC-07]](interaction-ux.md#perc-07) reserve a strong interrupt for a rare, critical, irreversible event; this row removes extra channels once the message is already stated. | What is the least attention this message needs? | S·w | [Microinteractions, ch. 4](../SOURCES.md#src-microinteractions-saffer) |

## 2. COG: Memory, cognitive load, decision cost<a name="fam-cog"></a>

| ID | Trigger | Rule | Answers | T·P | Src |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COG-01<a name="cog-01"></a> | A multi-step or voice flow requires holding more than ~4 novel, unrelated items off-screen | **Externalize working memory**: show the residue and current state, split long voice turns, and keep spoken option lists to ≤4–5; span beyond ~3–5 chunks drops the goal or a prior answer | What must the user hold that the UI does not show? | S·p | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| COG-02<a name="cog-02"></a> | A frequent action requires a typed command or buried recall with no chooser | **Recognition over recall**: let a broad audience see-or-hear-and-choose rather than recall-and-type; pure recall means non-discovery (↔ security; an authentication scheme that overloads recall gets written down or worked around) | Can a new user choose without remembering a string? | S·w | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| COG-03<a name="cog-03"></a> | The success path depends on the user noticing non-goal promo or chrome | **Design to the goal filter**: put goal-scented actions on the path and never require noticing orthogonal content; perception is filtered by the user's current goal, so off-goal cues are systematically missed | Does every required cue map to words of the user's goal? | B·p | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| COG-04<a name="cog-04"></a> | A high-stakes choice is worded as only gains or only losses, or forces mental calculation | **Support deliberate comparison**: for consequential choices, present a neutral or dual frame, side-by-side alternatives, unbiased data, and computed comparisons, and show assumptions; framing and wording can shift stated preferences, so give users support for deliberate evaluation (↔ [Principle 13]; ↔ [[GTM-01]](business-marketing.md#gtm-01) only a costly action is demand evidence before inventory commit; ↔ [[TEST-03]](engineering.md#test-03) pin actual behaviour before the edit commits a new contract; ↔ [[AIPX-01]](business-marketing.md#aipx-01) ship the heuristic baseline before the ML commitment) | Can the deliberate mind compare options without re-wording tricks? | B·p | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| COG-05<a name="cog-05"></a> | An environmental warning shows catastrophe imagery or disaster-only facts and names no response the audience can carry out | **Pair warning with agency**: connect the stated harm to something this audience can do at its own scale, because a disaster-only message can overwhelm people or lose their attention and leave anxiety without an action. | What can this audience do after seeing the warning? | S·p | [Design for a Better World, ch. 7](../SOURCES.md#src-design-for-a-better-world-norman) |
| COG-06<a name="cog-06"></a> | A product brief or acceptance list names current tasks but no user end condition | **Start from the user goal**: when the task list reflects the current tool or process, identify the user's end condition first and keep only the steps that still serve it, because automating inherited tasks can miss the purpose; task analysis remains useful after that goal analysis (↔ [[PROD-02]](business-marketing.md#prod-02) restate a solution brief as a measurable problem before prescribing a solution; ↔ [[COG-03]](interaction-ux.md#cog-03) put goal-scented actions on the path rather than relying on off-goal cues) | Why does the user do this task, and what end condition do they want? | S·p | [About Face: distilled, ch. 1](../SOURCES.md#src-about-face-4e-cooper) |
| COG-07<a name="cog-07"></a> | A required choice has options whose meaning is not clear at the decision point | **Just-in-time help**: when a choice cannot be made self-explanatory, put the briefest useful cue beside the decision and make that cue hard to miss, because an ambiguous option invites a wrong turn even when the step count is low; repeated paths and slow loads still make extra steps costly, and not every decision reduces to one simple choice (↔ [[FORM-03]](interaction-ux.md#form-03) a field hint does not cover a non-form option whose meaning is unclear) | Is the smallest useful explanation visible where this decision is made? | S·w | [Don't Make Me Think, Revisited, ch. 4](../SOURCES.md#src-dont-make-me-think-krug) |

## 3. NAV: Information architecture, wayfinding, information scent<a name="fam-nav"></a>

| ID | Trigger | Rule | Answers | T·P | Src |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| NAV-01<a name="nav-01"></a> | Hierarchy depth exceeds two levels for a general audience, with no persistent path | **Broad-shallow with a location path**: prefer breadth over depth and show a persistent "you are here" breadcrumb; depth loses the user's place and goal in working memory | Can the user answer "where am I and how deep?" from the chrome alone? | S·p | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| NAV-02<a name="nav-02"></a> | A menu, toolbar, or list silently reorders, or a function moves between steps | **Stable positions**: keep learnable items in the same place and the same function in the same place across steps; re-cue any move loudly, or every use becomes a fresh linear search (↔ eng [[NAME-04]](engineering.md#name-04) consistency beats local quality) | Does item order or control position change without the user asking? | S·w | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| NAV-03<a name="nav-03"></a> | A prominent visual cue (hero, large colour field) changes on the same route with no section signature | **Stable place cues**: keep a stable per-section visual signature and don't thrash prominent "where" cues without navigation, or a large visual change reads as a new page | Would a large visual change be mistaken for a page change? | S·r | [Designing with the Mind in Mind](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-with-the-mind-in-mind) |
| NAV-04<a name="nav-04"></a> | Visual design is signed off before a site map or screen-type inventory exists | **IA before presentation**: freeze visual lock-in until the structure, labels, nav model, and screen types exist, or a pretty UI hard-codes a taxonomy that blocks finding and doing (↔ design [[LAY-08]](design-aesthetics.md#lay-08) content-out before moodboard; ↔ biz [[PROD-02]](business-marketing.md#prod-02) problem before solution) | Is there a site map and screen-type list before visual sign-off? | S·p | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-05<a name="nav-05"></a> | Top-level nav labels have overlapping homes or a live task with no home | **MECE categories**: make primary categories mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive; a dual-home or a gap means the user cannot choose where to look or file (↔ grph [[GRPH-22]](graph-theory.md#grph-22); ↔ [[CLM-03]](business-marketing.md#clm-03) policy text and implementation must share one home; ↔ [[TEAM-01]](engineering.md#team-01) an architectural split the org does not communicate across is a boundary only on the diagram) | Can every in-scope item name exactly one primary nav home? | S·p | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-06<a name="nav-06"></a> | A high-frequency action is buried as deep as rare settings, or its only trigger is a hidden menu, an unlabeled icon, or a gesture away from the activity that needs it | **Frequency elevates chrome**: put frequent actions where that activity occurs, in global or top chrome when the action is global, and bury rare actions behind disclosure, because daily work otherwise pays a permanent search or recall tax. Keep a safety or legal control visible even when it is rare. An invisible gesture may supplement a visible path for experts, but it is a poor sole path for a broad audience. ↔ [[COG-02]](interaction-ux.md#cog-02) let people see and choose rather than recall a gesture or a buried command. | Where does this audience perform the frequent action, and is its only trigger hidden, unlabeled, or a gesture? | S·p | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-07<a name="nav-07"></a> | A wizard, modal, 404, or deep-link landing offers no path back to full navigation | **Escape Hatch required**: from any limited-nav or out-of-context screen, offer one labelled return to a known place, or the user is trapped and abandons | From this screen, is there one obvious out to a known place? | B·w | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-08<a name="nav-08"></a> | A first-run or home screen has no dominant task actions amid dense chrome | **Clear Entry Points**: present a few plain-language front-door actions and demote secondary links, or new and infrequent users hit "now what?" paralysis (↔ [[RLSE-04]](engineering.md#rlse-04) empty/first-time are designed states not blanks; ↔ [[CAL-02]](ml-systems.md#cal-02) unknown is a valid result; a void is not a forced guess; ↔ [[STRAT-03]](business-marketing.md#strat-03) too-tough is a designed basket not fake fluency) | Do new users see a few obvious start doors? | S·r | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-09<a name="nav-09"></a> | A linear wizard, checkout, or onboarding has no step map | **Progress Indicator on linear flows**: show the ordered, named steps with current and remaining, or users cannot estimate the finish or re-enter a prior step | Does each step show how far is left and what the steps are named? | S·w | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-10<a name="nav-10"></a> | A large catalog or content product offers only search or only browse on entry | **Feature, Search, and Browse**: a large item set needs all three entry modes, or known-item and exploratory users each lose half their find strategies | Can both a known-item and an exploratory user start without help? | S·p | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-11<a name="nav-11"></a> | A shareable or restorable view (map, filter, doc) does not round-trip through the URL | **Deep Links encode place and state**: put location and critical view params in the URL (excluding preference-stomping state), or the view cannot be shared or restored | Does opening the URL restore the same place and view? | S·w | [Designing Interfaces, 3rd ed.](../SOURCES.md#src-designing-interfaces) |
| NAV-12<a name="nav-12"></a> | Novel primary chrome breaks category/platform convention without tested need; or big-bang redesign with no familiar parallel path | **Match transferred conventions**: when users bring models from other products → start from common patterns; stage or opt-in any major break → else mental-model mismatch, abandon, competitor switch | Does this path match how peer products do the same job, or is the break user-tested? | S·p | [Laws of UX, ch. 1](../SOURCES.md#src-laws-of-ux) |
| NAV-13<a name="nav-13"></a> | User-facing nav, content, or help labels freeze while teams use different words for one concept, or jargon lacks outsider definitions / don't-say list | **Controlled vocabulary before label freeze**: when product language is multi-stakeholder or user-facing → publish preferred terms, don't-say list, and short definitions; kill accidental synonyms; vet with users → else findability and collaboration fail under false shared language | Is there a say/don't-say list, and would an outsider parse the primary labels? | S·p | [How to Make Sense of Any Mess, ch. 4](../SOURCES.md#src-how-to-make-sense-of-any-mess) |
| NAV-14<a name="nav-14"></a> | Primary categories follow expert/scientific taxonomy that fails lay card-sort or label comprehension | **Classify for the audience's mental model**: when the audience is not the expert class → prefer common mental-model labels and homes (dual-list if expert model also required) → else users cannot find everyday items | Would a target user put this item where we filed it on first try? | S·p | [How to Make Sense of Any Mess, ch. 6](../SOURCES.md#src-how-to-make-sense-of-any-mess) |
| NAV-15<a name="nav-15"></a> | A long reference text is one uninterrupted reading path, with no headings, links, anchors, or excerpts to the sections a reader needs. | **Text has entry and exit paths**: add headings, links, anchors, or excerpts so a reader can enter, locate, or leave without reading every preceding section, because a reference task otherwise has to pass through unrelated material. ↔ [[NAV-08]](interaction-ux.md#nav-08) a few front-door actions on a first-run screen do not give a long text internal jumps. ↔ [[A11Y-07]](accessibility.md#a11y-07) headings that are shown visually must also be programmatic; this row is the reader's path through the writing. | Can a reader reach the needed part without reading every preceding section? | S·w | [Thinking with Type: distilled, ch. 2](../SOURCES.md#src-lupton-thinking-with-type) |
| NAV-16<a name="nav-16"></a> | A spatial instruction, annotation, query, or model uses a relation such as left, behind, in front, near, or inside without stating whether the anchor is the viewer, the object, or the environment. | **Name the frame of reference**: record the viewer-centered, object-centered, or environment-centered anchor, and allow those frames to combine, because the same words can place the figure in different locations when the anchors disagree. ↔ [[NAV-12]](interaction-ux.md#nav-12) start from a transferred product convention; this row names which spatial anchor that convention uses. ↔ [[PERC-04]](interaction-ux.md#perc-04) resolve a figure that admits two visual parses; this row is an underspecified spatial phrase. | Which observer, object, or environmental direction anchors this relation? | S·p | [Spatial Semiotics and Spatial Mental Models, ch. 2](../SOURCES.md#src-thiering-spatial-semiotics) |
| NAV-17<a name="nav-17"></a> | A description of orientation in a lived landscape uses only generic coordinates or labels and omits the local place names, landmarks, or practised route cues. | **Anchor orientation in local cues**: name the places, landmarks, and route practice that community uses to keep a course, because those points carry orientation that a generic coordinate does not. Treat the anchors as findings about that landscape and community, not as a template for every culture. ↔ [[NAV-11]](interaction-ux.md#nav-11) put map location and view state in a URL; this row names the landmarks a spatial description of a landscape uses. ↔ [[NAV-14]](interaction-ux.md#nav-14) prefer the audience's category labels; this row names the landmarks that spatial description uses for orientation. | Which named or practised landmarks does this landscape's course actually use? | S·p | [Spatial Semiotics and Spatial Mental Models, ch. 6](../SOURCES.md#src-thiering-spatial-semiotics) |
| NAV-18<a name="nav-18"></a> | One surface is asked to host sustained primary work, a brief side task, and quiet background status as the same posture. | **Fit posture to work cadence**: give sustained primary work a sovereign workspace, a short supporting action a transient surface, and background work a quiet status that offers detail when summoned, because one product-wide posture either steals workspace or interrupts the primary task. ## 8. UXR: Usability research, metrics, and validation<a name="fam-uxr"></a>

Governs *what to measure, how to collect it without biasing it, how many participants, and how to report it honestly*: the human-measurement analogue of ml-systems [`EVAL`](ml-systems.md#fam-eval). Fires at **plan** time (pick the metric, design the study) and **review** time (analyse and report). Borders [`HAI`](interaction-ux.md#fam-hai) (evidence and uncertainty on a decision surface) and ml-systems [`EVAL`](ml-systems.md#fam-eval) (match the measure to the claim).

| UXR-01<a name="uxr-01"></a> | A result is labelled "validated" without naming which nested level it tested, downstream success is read as proof of an upstream claim, or ordinary use is inferred from still images or idealized clips alone | **Match claim to validation level**: bind each claim and its method to the same level of the four (domain / abstraction / idiom / algorithm); a wall-clock timing does not validate an encoding and a lab task does not validate domain fit, and a downstream success never proves an upstream block was right. ## Cross-lexicon links<a name="9-cross-lexicon-links"></a>

Interaction & UX borders the visual, implementation, accessibility, and model layers it sits between:

- **Visual tokens (engineering `UI-*`, design `LAY/COL`).** `UI-*` owns the metric: spacing scale, contrast ratio, type ramp; PERC owns the **meaning** those metrics carry. `PERC-01` (proximity) and `INT-03` (button grouping) are the perceptual reason behind spacing and grouping tokens; a value-first palette `[COL-04]` is what lets figure-ground and pop survive greyscale.
- **Accessibility is a hard constraint on every row.** Visual hierarchy must also be programmatic (`PERC-01`/`PERC-03`), pop and status must not be colour-only (`PERC-05`/`PERC-06`), item tools and forms must be operable without a mouse and with real labels (`INT-04`, `FORM-03`, `FORM-06`). Where a UX rule and an [`A11Y`](accessibility.md#fam-a11y) rule fire together, the accessibility one is the floor, not a trade.
- **Grouping is one audit in two lanes (↔ [`GRPH`](graph-theory.md#fam-grph)).** `PERC-03` (Gestalt audit) and `NAV-05` (MECE) are `[GRPH-22]` (partition-plus-verification) in perceptual and IA form: propose a grouping, then verify it invents no false relation. A dual-home nav label fails the same audit as a dual-cluster membership.
- **Correction, preview, and undo meet ml-systems.** `HAI-02` (correction reaches the source), `INT-09` (reversible command stack), and `INT-07` (preview before commit) are the human-surface form of the model-side obligations in `[PROV-01]` and `[CAL-02]`; the families meet on the review surface where a person acts on a model claim. `HAI-01`/`HAI-06` are `[PROV-01]` and `[Principle 9]` seen from the UI.
- **Memory and consent are security surfaces (↔ [`SEC`](security.md#fam-sec)).** `COG-02` (recognition over recall) is why unusable authentication fails as a control: an overloaded recall burden becomes written-down or weak credentials; `FORM-04` (no dark defaults) keeps a consent boundary from being crossed by an unchosen default.
- **Claims discipline.** `HAI-05` (imperceptible AI) and `HAI-09` (trustworthy ≠ trusted) are `[CLM-05]` (a claim needs inspectable evidence) applied to automation.
- **Encoding builds on perception and borders graph and accessibility (↔ [`PERC`](interaction-ux.md#fam-perc), [`GRPH`](graph-theory.md#fam-grph), [`A11Y`](accessibility.md#fam-a11y)).** `VIZ-*` is the layer above [`PERC`](interaction-ux.md#fam-perc): PERC asks *can the eye see it*, VIZ asks *does the encoding match the data and the query*. `VIZ-05` extends `PERC-06` (single-target pop) to multi-class search; `VIZ-16` extends `PERC-05` (fovea) to query layout; `VIZ-07` (survive greyscale) is the [`A11Y`](accessibility.md#fam-a11y) non-colour floor from the data side; `VIZ-13` (node-link grammar) draws what `GRPH-*` lays out. [`VIZ`](interaction-ux.md#fam-viz) does not own uncertainty display: `HAI-08` does.
- **Measurement is the human twin of model evaluation (↔ ml-systems [`EVAL`](ml-systems.md#fam-eval), [`HAI`](interaction-ux.md#fam-hai)).** `UXR-01`/`UXR-03` (validate at the level of the claim; measure the goal, not a proxy) are `[EVAL-01]` and `[Principle 4]` for human studies; `UXR-05`/`UXR-15` (report the interval; show the composite's components) are `[HAI-08]` (uncertainty at the decision granularity) seen from the research side. Where a UX number will drive a decision, [`UXR`](interaction-ux.md#fam-uxr) governs whether it was valid to report; the writing lexicon governs how it is phrased.

## Consumption<a name="10-consumption"></a>

Referenced by ID at the decision point, never read front-to-back. `PERC-*` fire in a **review** pass over a screenshot: greyscale, blur, and unlabeled-grouping inspection catch most violations; `COG-*` and `NAV-*` fire at **plan** time on flows, IA, and forms; `INT-*`/`FORM-*` at **write** time on actions and inputs; `HAI-*` on any surface where a human acts on a model output; `VIZ-*` fire on a **chart or encoding**: greyscale, data-to-channel mapping, and a "which query does this serve" check catch most violations; `UXR-*` fire at **plan** time on a study design and metric choice and at **review** time on a results table or report. A CSS or component diff routes to `PERC + UI + A11Y`; a form to `FORM + A11Y`; a chart or timeline to `VIZ + PERC + A11Y`; a cluster-review screen to `VIZ + PERC + HAI + GRPH + ml-systems`; a usability study or metrics deck to `UXR + HAI + ml-systems [EVAL]`. A consuming harness inlines the trigger plus the question by phase tag and cites the ID; the mechanism is not inlined into the harness.
