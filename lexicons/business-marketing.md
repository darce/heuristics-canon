# Business & Marketing Heuristics Lexicon

## About this document

Decision rules for strategy, product validation, go-to-market, negotiation, bootstrap
economics, and claims. Referenced, not read end to end: a roadmap, scope, pricing page,
or outbound sequence cites a rule by ID where the decision happens, and this file holds
the claim behind it.

Weight falls on Drummond and Cauty's
[*The Manual (How to Have a Number One the Easy Way)*](../SOURCES.md#src-klf-the-manual),
Munger's [*Poor Charlie's Almanack*](../SOURCES.md#src-poor-charlies-almanack),
Berg's [*Porn Work*](../SOURCES.md#src-porn-work), and Voss and Raz's
[*Never Split the Difference*](../SOURCES.md#src-never-split-the-difference), with
Ferriss's [*The 4-Hour Workweek*](../SOURCES.md#src-four-hour-workweek) on bootstrap
economics. A non-trivial share of rows is unsourced practice rather than a named work.

| Trigger | observable in a roadmap, metric, conversation, or market signal |
| Rule | the falsifiable claim: condition, action, consequence |
| Answers | the one question to ask before the decision is made |
| T·P | tier and phase, below |
| Src | source slug, resolved in [`SOURCES.md`](../SOURCES.md) |

Tier: **B**locker (existential or irreversible if violated), **S**hould (strong default
with named exemptions), **J**udgment (weigh in context, and escalate when the context is
missing). Phase: **s**trategy, **p**roduct, **g**tm, **o**ps.

**Example of a row that does not meet B:** [STRAT-13](#strat-13) is S: comparing
income after the stated floor is met improves a tradeoff, but the choice is not
therefore existential or irreversible.

## Cross-source tensions worth keeping<a name="9-cross-source-tensions-worth-keeping"></a>

- **Conviction vs falsification**: [[STRAT-07]](business-marketing.md#strat-07) (Karp: don't let the crowd veto) ↔ [[PROD-04]](business-marketing.md#prod-04) (Lean UX: evidence kills darlings). Resolution by object: *values and positioning* run on conviction; *features and funnels* run on falsification.
- **Novelty vs precision**: [[GTM-08]](business-marketing.md#gtm-08) (novelty wins first exposure) ↔ [[AIPX-07]](business-marketing.md#aipx-07) (Ameisen: precision-first for advice products). First exposure can be novel; the *output* can never be sloppy in a trust product.
- **Niche membership vs consequential problems**: [[GTM-03]](business-marketing.md#gtm-03) (Ferriss: sell to a tribe you belong to) ↔ [[STRAT-06]](business-marketing.md#strat-06) (Karp: work on what matters). A rare wedge satisfies both; hunt for it.
- **Novelty vs the regulatory line**: [[GTM-08]](business-marketing.md#gtm-08) (novelty beats craft at first exposure) ↔ [[CLM-02]](business-marketing.md#clm-02) (the cost asymmetry is absolute). The hook can be as loud as the campaign wants; the claim inside it stays on the safe side of the line. The novelty lives in the packaging, not in the claim.
- **Channel contract vs claims review**: [[PROD-09]](business-marketing.md#prod-09) (write the gatekeeper's contract before the components) and [[CLM-05]](business-marketing.md#clm-05) (three hostile readers) are the same move at different stakes: App Store guidelines, FDA wellness lines, and radio-edit rules are all channel contracts read by someone paid to find violations. The same pattern appears in playlist committees and app review.
- **Boring releases vs loud launches**: [[GTM-08]](business-marketing.md#gtm-08)/[[GTM-13]](business-marketing.md#gtm-13) stunts ↔ eng [[RLSE-07]](engineering.md#rlse-07) (phased rollout, stop criteria). The marketing moment can be theatrical, but the rollout percentage is set by the monitoring plan, never by the stunt calendar.
